# orient/S20 — scalable LARRY transport

## Summary

Status: **done**. S20 prepared the full read-only LARRY input (130,887 cells; 2,000 HVGs; PCA-30 fit on all cells with randomized PCA, `random_state=0`) and evaluated dense POT, low-rank moscot ranks 50/200, and minibatch POT transport. The best scalable solver is **minibatch**.

Both gates pass. G1 passes at 4,000 cells/day, and G2 completes full-LARRY minibatch OT plus velocity/flow-J Jacobian within the 14 GB and 2-hour per-unit limits. The dense full-LARRY OT unit was skipped because the larger consecutive-day cost matrix alone is approximately 21.0 GB at float64, before the transport plan and solver workspace.

## What ran

- Verified the binding LARRY checksum: `3e13ff16d0f3d1338932860abe15b1609749bc7f70d4b2596058ae3d7763a80e`.
- Fit the all-cell PCA-30 transport space from backed HDF5 chunks, with library normalization to target 2249 followed by `log1p`, then nested `default_rng(0)` samples at 2,000/4,000/8,000/16,000 cells per day.
- Ran dense POT Sinkhorn, low-rank moscot rank 50 and rank 200, and minibatch POT with exactly ten source-batch appearances per source cell. MeasuredDrift used `MeasuredDrift(30, seed=0)` defaults and autodiff Jacobians.
- Ran agreement against dense OT at 2,000/4,000/8,000 cells/day, scaling at 2,000/4,000/8,000/16,000/full where feasible, and full-LARRY minibatch velocity plus flow-J lambda1 by cell type/day.
- One fit was first launched with the low-rank environment by mistake and failed before analysis because Torch was unavailable there; the permitted retry used `.sandbox/bin/python` and completed. No result depended on the failed attempt.

### Scaling highlights

The complete 36-row OT/fit timing and memory table is in [`scaling.csv`](../../../benchmarks/scaling/scaling.csv). At 16,000/day, dense OT peaked at 9.94 GB; minibatch OT peaked at 2.47 GB and took 126.79 s. Full minibatch OT took 290.72 s and full fitting took 1,278.34 s. All completed units were below 14 GB and two hours.

Full-LARRY minibatch lambda1 was summarized for 11 cell types across days. The median cell-type comparison with S11 4,000/day measured-OT lambda1 has Spearman rho `0.818182`.

## Deviations

- Dense full-LARRY OT was not run because the 48,498 × 54,140 float64 pairwise distance matrix is approximately 21.0 GB before solver workspace, exceeding the 14 GB per-process limit.
- The first LARRY preparation attempt hit a local backed-CSR indexing incompatibility before reading the full matrix. The unit was retried once using direct chunked HDF5 CSR reads; the retry completed and is the binding preparation result.
- The first `fit_lowrank_r50_2000` command used `.venv-baselines` and stopped immediately because Torch is not installed there. It was retried once in `.sandbox`; the successful retry is used in `scaling.csv`.

## Observations

- Minibatch OT is the only tested scalable solver satisfying G1; both low-rank moscot configurations show poor velocity and lambda1 agreement with dense OT.
- Minibatch agreement remains near one through 8,000/day, while its runtime and memory scale more gently than dense OT.
- Full-LARRY minibatch lambda1 is positive for nearly all cell-type/day groups; the all-cell output and per-group counts are retained in `larry_full_lambda1.csv`.

## Suggestions

- Use minibatch POT as the scalable LARRY transport baseline for subsequent orientation analyses, retaining dense OT only at sampled sizes where its memory estimate is admissible.
- If low-rank OT is revisited, investigate the coupling/velocity discrepancy before using it for flow-J spectral conclusions.
