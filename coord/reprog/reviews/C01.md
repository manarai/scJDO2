## Interpretation

**Result.** I1 PASS, I2 PASS (661 multi-day clones). Inventory done; no fits.

**Meaning for C02.**
- **Clone fate cannot come from published outcomes**: only 8 clones (2,647 cells) carry them, too few for a held-out test. A clone-fate test has to define fate from each clone's own late-day cells (LARRY-style). Two limits: clones are small (median 5 cells), and author states cover only 32,717 of 85,010 CellTag cells, so "late-day state" needs a state call for unannotated cells, made without the clone labels and fixed before the test. The 272 clones spanning ≤ d12 and ≥ d21 are the realistic test set; C02 should count how many keep ≥ 1 annotated late cell.
- **Factors (the workstream's design issue):**
  - CellTag: the transgene is a separate joint feature `FoxA1.HNF4a` (one bicistronic feature, not two). It is ~0 at day 0 and 3.5–8.7 CP10k in 77–88% of cells from day 3 on, while endogenous `Hnf4a` and `Foxa1` stay near zero (< 0.01 CP10k, < 1% of cells) through day 28. So the endogenous genes can be used as candidate drivers without transgene contamination, but they barely move; the "known factor ranked early" check is not meaningful for iEP and should be dropped or replaced.
  - Schiebinger: there is no separate cassette feature, so OKSM reads cannot be split into transgene and endogenous. The known-factor check on OKSM is confounded and should not be a gate.
- Schiebinger's broad cell sets (MET etc.) are partly OT-derived; use them as covariates, not as validation truth (executor's point, adopted).

**Caveats.** CellTag annotations cover a subset; Schiebinger DiPSC controls (14,918 cells, no numeric day) excluded; some download byte-range hashes are range hashes, not whole-object hashes (recorded as such).

**Executor's observations.** All checked ones hold (outcome count, joint feature, OT-derived sets). Suggestions adopted: separate multi-day coverage from outcome coverage in C02; treat the cassette and the reference-gene features as distinct classes; OT sets as covariates.
